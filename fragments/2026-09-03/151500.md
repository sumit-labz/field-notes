# Typography Poster Process — Living Document

Source method: Paula Scher's typographic process (`PaulaScherTypographicProcess.md`). This doc evolves as we work — update it, don't fork it.

Dual purpose: personal typography practice AND a real candidate for Stencil + Frame brand marketing, if it earns that place through evidence.

## Piece 1 — LOCKED

Phrase: "Brands have a color palette where a reason to exist should be." (Sealed hero line, `BRAND_BOOK_COPY_v0.md`)

Why this phrase: it names the problem, not the solution — a founder has swatches where they should have an answer. Structurally a substitution, which gives typography/image an actual mechanism to illustrate.

Stage 0 research (Pass C — personal/observed material):

- Images: swatches, physical palettes, colourful boxes, bags, stationery, packaging, billboards, TV ads, websites
- Accusation: design reduced to colourful boxes and logos; speed replaces a slower design process; AI/Canva makes people believe they can generate a brand before understanding it
- Personal observation: "They see Canva and think they can start building marketing posters."

Expanded word pass — three metaphor families, not one list:

- **Decoration** (surface dressed up, nothing underneath): palette, swatch, veneer, gloss, finish, coat, wrapper, packaging → visual grammar: color/texture layered over or instead of real content.
- **Disguise** (something worn to pass as what it's not): costume, imposter, mask, facade, borrowed, counterfeit, mimicry, stand-in → visual grammar: something covering or lifted to reveal what's underneath. Maps directly onto the already-locked Correction device.
- **Shortcut** (speed and process integrity traded away): shortcut, outsourced, premature, instant, template, autopilot, fast-track, corner-cut → visual grammar: motion marks, timestamps, conveyor-belt/assembly-line feel, before/after speed comparisons. Closest to the personal observation.

Decision needed at Stage 1: thumbnail across all three families, or pick one to commit to before sketching. Not yet decided.

LOCKED: Decoration / palette family. Focal fragments: "color palette" (dominant, oversized) vs. "reason to exist" (small, cramped, nearly buried). Stage 1 design problem: represent "palette-ness" using only monochrome shape/form (chip outlines, blank swatch grids, color-wheel silhouette) since color itself is off-limits until Stage 5. Grid layout is a strong natural fit (swatch cards are already gridded objects). Risk to watch at Stage 2: this is the most literal/obvious reading of the phrase — needs the shape-work to earn it, or it risks reading as generic "branding = colorful boxes" content.

## The Pipeline (v2 — research-first, styling deferred)

Key principle: two different kinds of constraint, not one.

- Working constraint (Stage 1): provisional, arbitrary, exists only to prevent overwhelm during idea generation — e.g. monochrome only. This matches Scher's own advice to limit palette/type "at first," but the limit is a study discipline, not the final brand decision.
- Brand execution constraint (Stage 5): the real, locked Stencil + Frame values — exact Fraunces settings, exact gold hex, material/device choice. Deferred until a composition has earned it.

### Stage 0 — Research

Study the phrase itself before touching a layout. Write down: keywords (substitution, swatch, palette, absence, standing-in-for), imagery associations (paint chips, color wheels, blank forms, placeholders), what the line is actually accusing (decoration mistaken for decision). Treat this as fodder, not homework — Scher's "slot machine" needs material to shuffle. No layout decisions yet.

### Stage 1 — Hand thumbnailing (working constraint only)

Working constraint: monochrome (graphite/black ink), hand-drawn letterforms only, no brand color or specific typeface commitment yet. One thumbnail per layout system — axial, grid, random/asymmetrical, dilatational — 4–8 total, fast, index-card size. Pure composition and idea testing: where does the "palette standing in for purpose" substitution actually become visible through arrangement?

### Stage 2 — Present & review

Bring the thumbnails here (photos or descriptions). We pattern-read them together against one question: does the layout embody the substitution, or just hold the words? Narrow to 2–3 candidates worth developing further. No styling discussion yet — composition only.

### Stage 3 — Claude Design: composition variations

Feed the 2–3 candidates to Claude Design for layout variations. Still provisional style (basic type, minimal color) — this stage is testing composition and arrangement at a cleaner resolution, not producing a finished look. Resist the urge to "make it look good" here; that's Stage 5.

### Stage 4 — Decide direction

Pick the one composition worth carrying forward. Locked from here — no new compositions introduced downstream.

### Stage 5 — Styling (brand execution, now it's earned)

Now lock the real values: Fraunces (exact variable axes), gold #c8a96e as accent, bg #080f0a, coral reserved (mascot only). Choose material/texture — graphite, ink, paper grain, scan artifacts — and which device from `ART_DIRECTION_SKILL.md` fits (The Correction is the natural fit for this phrase, since it's already structured as one). Decide here, not earlier, whether an image is needed at all (see Image Selection Protocol below).

### Stage 6 — Develop & digitize

Hand-letter the winning composition at scale in the chosen style. Scan, vector-refine, adjust spacing/kerning. If an image layer was decided in Stage 5, source or generate it now (protocol below) and integrate.

### Stage 7 — Review

Scher's question: does the final piece look like what it is? If not, iterate at Stage 6 — don't reopen Stage 1–4 without new evidence from the work itself.

## Image Selection Protocol

Step 1 — Decide at Stage 5, not before: does this piece need an image, or does the lettering carry it alone? Both are legitimate in Scher's practice. Don't default to yes.

Step 2 — If yes, order of preference:

1. Found/physical — real paint chips, a real swatch card, photographed or collaged by hand. Matches Process-as-Provenance directly.
2. Hand-illustrated — drawn/painted by you, same register as the lettering.
3. AI-generated (fal.ai/OpenRouter) — last resort, only for a specific visual gap neither of the above can fill, and only after the composition is fully locked (Stage 6, never earlier).

Step 3 — Non-negotiable constraint on AI-generated images: Stencil + Frame's visual territory is explicitly anti-generic-AI-polish. Any AI-generated image must be run through the same hand-treatment as the rest of the device library — scanned, cropped imperfectly, marked, aged — never used raw. If it can't take that treatment convincingly, it's the wrong image.

Step 4 — Selection criteria: ties to the phrase's specific mechanism (not generic brand mood); doesn't duplicate what the type already says; fits the device budget (max 3 per piece); one image element per piece as a rule.
