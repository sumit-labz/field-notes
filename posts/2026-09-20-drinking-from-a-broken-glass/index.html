Drinking From a Broken Glass: Hyperfixation and Acceptance

Sep 20, 2026 · 4 min read · Being Seen

Listen 0:00
a borrowed voice reads this (AI-generated narration)

Why does it feel that the best days are also the worst days? I was examining my own behavior for the last few days. I was super excited about creating drum beats, so amazing that I could spend hours and hours just building beats and music. And I was getting more and more hyper-fixated after creating music. Anything I was doing, I was searching for something that would completely involve me. I was building a desktop app for myself to put all my shortcuts in one place: minor-level programming, minor-level planning, not too deep. And I kept getting back to it, wanting to improve it, build it, the Claude Code routine. This looks good, making widgets. And I found it so fascinating that you are the driver and the machine builds the software for you. Something so amazing.

While all this was going on, and when I was coming out of the work, I was finding it hard to relate with people. On one side, I was thinking, wow, I found something that I would love to do again and again, and even if I grow old, I will have things which will interest me like no one else. Most people are bored by consumption. Sometimes I also felt how boring it would be to just consume content. This is what happens to most people: we end up just consuming, although it gives a sense of superiority, that I always have something to do.

I realized that while I was in the work, I would go outside and tell people I discovered this. Wow, this is so amazing. I would call people to my desk and show them how amazing beat creation is. But at the same time, I was finding it hard to relate with people. I just wanted to go back to work again and again. While I was feeling, this is the best day of my life and I'm enjoying the work I'm doing, there was also guilt coming in: you might have learned something great and be able to create something great, but when you are failing to relate with people, what is the success worth?

While having a dialogue with myself, I was thinking, what is wrong with me? I'm not looking people in the eye. I'm not talking about this. I'm not exposing my vulnerability. I was giving so many excuses to myself for why it is like that. I'm not able to understand why I can't relate with people, why I can't have a deep conversation with someone, why I'm not excited about calling people the way I used to, having fun, party. That approach was completely missing in me, and I was like, this is the best and the worst day of my life, where I could feel both.

Then I slept over it for a whole night, and in the morning I asked AI how to get out of hyper-fixation. The answer it gave seems pretty convincing: you have to close the loop when you close the session, what you did and what you're going to pick next. After that, give yourself a rest, then accept that you will have a hard time relating with people. Then slowly get back to talking to someone. I'm going to try that and make it part of my system. But somehow the solution seems convincing, and at the same time I'm doubting whether I'll lose my hyperfocus itself.

I sat on this problem and was unable to solve it. Last night I saw a dream where a woman was drinking something from a broken glass. A person I knew, an acquaintance, not a friend or something. She was drinking from it, and at the same time my mind was explaining that this glass had been made to work. When I woke up in the morning and analyzed my dream, I realized most likely this is hyperfixation itself. You are drinking from a broken glass and you are making it functional. And I think this is the metaphor for acceptance in ADHD: things are broken and still you are making it work.

Things are broken, and still you are making it work.

I continued to think about this broken glass metaphor. I also realized that in general our attitude in life is: if something is broken, just throw it away. I think I've read somewhere about Japanese craft, that when something is broken, they join the pieces and keep it in the showcase or something. I'm not sure, but it seems to be a valid idea. If you are broken, what would you do? You will not throw yourself away. You will not throw away that part, right? You will still use it. And I think that's what the woman was doing: nourishing herself from something broken. It seems like such a great idea.

If you are broken, you will not throw yourself away.
