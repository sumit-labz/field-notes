This morning, I woke with a clarity emerging. While lying in bed for an hour—wanting to sleep but unable to—the chatter came so fast that I had no control over it. But here's the thing: suddenly, what happened yesterday started making sense. How to build the new identity. How to move forward.

The good thing that happened yesterday was posting on my website, this website, Field Notes. And that's when it hit me: this *is* the operating system. This is where I document things and take them further. The identity has to be fixed here. From here, I reach to new places—Substack, maybe a YouTube brand. All the work happens here.

Studio Zero is a temporary name I've chosen, but here's the origin story: when I came out of my job, owning a studio was on my wish list. Working *as* a studio, delivering designs. I'm not sure if I'll be working with particular clients or developing products that may or may not succeed. But I think what matters more right now is doing experiments.

I have one or two experiments in the pipeline: the Stencil and the Frame, and the Story Dojo. I'm excited about both, but I overthought the Stencil and the Frame, so I'm taking a pause there. Luckily, I have Story Dojo where I can invest my creative energy right now.

As I begin this Studio Zero journey, the first thing I have to solve is distribution itself. And I need to find a sweet spot between distribution and experimentation. I can justify that I'm building a product for myself, but I also wonder: will it be useful for others? How will I show it to them?

For an ADHD brain to arrive at clarity, it comes through a lot of writing. So one of the practices—obsessions—I'm adding today is writing practice itself. A few years back, I did a course with Shani Raja on editing, on writing with intent. It takes a long time to do or edit something properly, but it's so good for an ADHD brain. It brings the thought clarity that doesn't come naturally to a brain like mine. So I want to invest time in learning editing skills, because what I've learned recently is this: if you want to start distribution, you have to start with writing or speaking. I'm not comfortable with YouTube yet, but writing? Definitely. I can put up a Substack post.

For the next week, I want to dedicate time to setting up an editing practice where I can write. I'm uncertain about using AI because authorship should always be mine. As I edit more, I think I'll be diving deep into the concept of authorship in the age of AI. Because if I master the editing, maybe I can automate some processes, or maybe AI can support me. But for now, I'm just going to close this note with a simple intention: let's prepare this week to set up the writing practice. And also give some thought to how I want to approach the YouTube channel for Story Dojo.
