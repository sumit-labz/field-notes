Spontaneity

Sep 3, 2026 · 2 min read · Being Seen

Listen 0:00
a borrowed voice reads this (AI-generated narration)

I'm reflecting on what happened this morning, and somehow I feel spontaneity took over. I had the right tools at the right time to do something amazing. I was writing the premise, and suddenly I realized that if I could visualize it — maybe a board in my room — then why not create an HTML? That would be the most flexible tool. I worked with Claude and ChatGPT to create an initial design for the premise stage. While I was working on the Claude design, it produced something awesome that anybody could use. I realized I could open source the design and show it to people. It's an amazing tool that can export the story structure and develop scenes with the help of AI.

When I say the help of AI, I mean authorship. The authorship has to be mine. It doesn't mean that AI will generate the scene for you — AI can give you ideas. There were so many times I was stuck developing the premise because I'm new to story development. Design principle matters a lot in story writing, and I spent a week discussing with AI as a co-thinking partner to unblock myself.

In the coming time, I have to deeply introspect what authorship even means. AI can generate — but authorship has to be mine. The line is thin, and holding it is the whole job: what I make is mine because I choose to own it.

AI can generate — but authorship has to be mine.

Spontaneity could be such an amazing thing for an ADHD mind. If you have time and resources, spontaneity and curiosity can take you miles beyond what you could ever imagine. I could never imagine I was able to do it, but I allowed myself to be spontaneous.

Spontaneity and curiosity can take you miles beyond what you could ever imagine.

So here's the real question: next time spontaneity shows up uninvited, will I know how to use it — or just get lucky again?
