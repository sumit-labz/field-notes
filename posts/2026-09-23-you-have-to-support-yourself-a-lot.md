For a long time I was trying to build an app for myself, tackling the core problems of ADHD — something that brings me back to the same app, over and over. I designed it as a vibe-coded desktop app on a Rust-based framework. Initially I tried an HTML-bundled app, but it was consuming a lot of memory, and then I found a lower-memory way to build it.

Some of the principles I used for ADHD in this app: motivating myself by doing daily practices again and again, and showing a total of how many times I did each repetition. I think this is the core problem in ADHD, that practices become boring, but as the number increases, it gives you an idea on the top of how many reps you have already done.

I have shortcuts for my other GitHub applications that I can launch, because it was becoming very problematic to go inside every folder and do an NPM run dev.

Mostly I was starting my day off with a conflict. I want to do this. I want to do that. So what is the focus of today? Is it learning or is it creation? If it is learning, just log it in one sentence and do it.

One cool feature I built is music and the backgrounds. The music is something very calm, wind chimes, as well as some classical music. Claude Code pulled in these files, and I don't know where from, so they may not be commercially free.

And sticky notes. The notes can really go crazy. Although I use Obsidian for maintaining my notes, that's a separate thing. Here I just capture my sparks of ideas in the sticky notes.

What I love building about this app is that I have put in seven colors and seven different styles, like brutalism, neo-brutalism, and others.

I also built a widget for tracking the focus this week and next week. I try to hide it, and I'm not expanding on that much, because I just want to glance at it and put it back in a drawer. I think it's a good use of UX, and so far I'm liking it. I really want to expand on the notes side as well.

The core way this app is designed is widgets. Everything is like a desktop widget that can collapse and expand with a nice animation, and while the widget is collapsed, it gives you the summary of what is happening inside it. I love to play around and fidget with it when I'm free. It helps me organize my brain, it lets me fidget, and it's fun to have something good to play around with on your desktop. There is a way in the music player to make the background animation go really loud and respond to the rhythm of the music. Something really fun to vibe code, and I think vibe coding is now a new addiction for me.

I have also done some background nudging with animations. In the morning, the app subtly suggests a morning walk. In the evening, it suggests I step away from the desk and do something, reminding me in a very gentle way.

I love developing this app in my free time. I think the whole idea started when I read that you have to support yourself a lot, and that it is a good investment to tackle all those mental patterns. Once you have built a system, you can rely on it, and the system should give you free space.
