I picked up this habit of tarot cards a couple of years back. I tried different ways to use it, mostly to predict something. But the longer I've used it, the more I realize I'll always be confused about what it actually reveals. So I decided to use it as a divinational tool instead — a way to see where I'm stuck and where I could improve. Mostly for self-reflection.

I'd been feeling stuck the last few days — on the personal side, there's still a lot that hasn't changed in me. Things like speaking fluently, putting up a video camera and just talking, or showing up daily for practice in a way that feels fun. For an ADHD brain, that's a real challenge. I can't do a daily practice unless there's some creative challenge in it, or some kind of time constraint that makes it playful. I've been noticing a lot of things about myself that are stopping me from reaching the next level.

So I pulled a card and asked what's blocking me. I pulled the death card. I sat with it for a while — what does this mean to me right now? It made me conscious of all the gaps I just talked about. Even small things, like the fact that I have a standing desk, and every morning I still go straight to the chair and get stuck there, barely moving. That feeds into how I use the computer too — I stay still for three or four hours, hyperfixation kicks in, and I don't move.

As I said in an earlier post about hyperfixation, there are pros and cons to it. One con I keep noticing is a kind of cognitive fixation — I get stuck, unable to move. I keep feeling I should have more of an artist's approach: pick up the brush, apply color, stay quick and spontaneous, keep moving. That movement is missing from me, from my body. How can I be a good creator if my body doesn't move?

So these are the gaps I found in myself: not showing up for daily practice, sitting at the computer too long, not moving my body. Not doing the daily work that would build the skills I need for the future — composition, color, rhythm, motion, editing, writing, speaking. If I don't do it regularly, I won't get good at it. I think that's what the death card meant to me. A psychological death — just becoming aware of this.

There's other stuff too, in the personal life. I'm not someone who fully shows up with emotions, to anyone. And I think that's necessary — emotional health comes from how you express things. If you're not expressing, you're piling it up. Creating repressed emotions. So speaking up matters too. Writing gives me a space to put thoughts down, at least.

But how do I actually behave around people — do I speak up? I think that's one of the areas too. It feels like meeting the feminine qualities within myself — being loving, caring. There's no training for men in their 40s to shift that thinking and lean into it, because the culture keeps encouraging structure instead. And that structure doesn't really work — ADHD or not, I think society runs on masculine traits. But anyway, it's my own journey to find out.
