I am revisiting typography after a long time, and my ADHD brain is saying: how can you reach hyperfixation while pursuing it? So I started looking back — what were my other influences? The books I've read and the ideas they inspired. I got really excited about typography once, and pursued a lot of typographic layouts.

I came to know about the order in which typography should be structured: focal point, order, surprise, negative space, and the details to show. Since then I've practiced these elements without even remembering much of the theory, but I still remember picking up constraints, creating thumbnail layouts, and iterating until landing on the right combination.

{{fragment:2026-08-31-111113}}

At the beginning of the day I feel very confused. How do I start something so complex? And that's why I feel both excited and overwhelmed by the thought of getting back into typography, and how useful it is for having a statement for my personal brand.

My influences are definitely Paula Scher. I've seen her work, but I haven't fully read her book. Apart from that, Typography Essentials keeps me excited with its visual content. I think I need to train my brain to stay excited and enthusiastic about creating typographic layouts.

{{fragment:2026-08-31-111052}}

What I did was run some research using AI to figure out the process Paula Scher used in her work. It described her process well: typography as a slot machine. The fodder is what's in your mind — your influences, memories, things that inspire you. You take those elements and spin them until you arrive at the right combination.

I'll include my research on how she creates contemporary typography. Her work is displayed at MoMA, as well as the public art museums of New York.

So I've decided: this research, and a process driven by hand and input, should be the brief. Before wondering too much about what to create, I realized I'm already working on a product — Stencil & Frame — and its MVP. Let's think from that, and develop some posters for it.
