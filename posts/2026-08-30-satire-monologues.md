Okay, this is a pretty insane idea that has, you know, driven my life and I'm not able to throw it out anymore.

One of the ideas I got was when I watched *The Killer* at my friend's house. I didn't watch it fully, but it's a David Fincher movie — the guy who directed *Fight Club*. The dialogues were so amazing, and the guy sounds so true despite being a killer. He knows the rules of the system, he knows what the system is, and you're intrigued by why he's chosen such a path. Watching it took me in a different direction: can I create something like this — monologues?

I thought about this for a long time, but somehow satire started making sense to me. Satire feels beautiful — more than serious. People want to listen to absurdity right now. That makes more sense in today's era. Even the revolutions in the past leaned on absurdism and satire, because logic has reached its peak — logic doesn't serve anymore. I think that's where art movements like Dada picked up: they relied on absurdity because there's so much hustle culture, so much habit-building, so much pressure to be extremely logical just to get ahead in life. Satire started making more sense against all of that.

So I thought, okay, let's attempt it.

Today something clicked. My mind started wondering why I haven't read the book I ordered — John Truby's *The Anatomy of Story*. I've read maybe 20–30% of it. I still remember the section on writing a premise: he gives you two, three weeks just for that, encourages you to take your time.

I want to continue this — writing satire, short monologues, using Truby's method. I got a bit of a push from AI, which pointed out that Truby is deeply psychological, and that appeals to me a lot.

So let's see. Let me hop onto this journey — reading the book, writing the satire, finding out how hard it actually is. Can I write a monologue as good as *The Killer*? Let's give it a shot.

Kicking off this journey today.
