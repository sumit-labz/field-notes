I am feeling a sense of grief after getting into hyperfixation loops. The grief comes from an inability to connect with people. While I am continuously working, dreaming about big outcomes, working towards them, sometimes failing, sometimes succeeding, I am sensing that the structure which led to hyperfixation has stopped allowing people into my life too.

I was a very well-connected person before I went through these hyperfixation loops in maybe the last one month. I used to talk with people, and now I don't connect with people much. I used to give reasons that the need for constant connection is not worth it. But I can feel it now to my bones that due to hyperfixation, I have started distancing from people, and it feels awkward now. On one side I feel that yes, I have something to give, to dedicate my life to big dreams, but on the other hand I am feeling the pressure of distance and relationships.

Yesterday I was doing EFT therapy, and I realised that most of the pressure to perform is coming from gender expectation, from the society that I have always been living with. The inability to earn money could lead to so much frustration within myself. I have so much expectation from myself. I was doing the therapy to train my nervous system that the pressure is real, but it doesn't mean you'll stop living in a fun way.

Somewhere deep inside, two things are coming together: the insecurities of life when you don't earn money, and how you will safeguard your future. And the second thing is the hyperfixation disconnecting me from people. Both are operating, and I am aware that most people who have ADHD end up losing their job, and it's very difficult to go back to a job and earn money.

I was hesitating to say all this because I thought it would not look good in a public blog, but this is also a part, a phase of me that I am currently going through, and which may not be very important. I am not sure about that, but this is how I felt, and that's most important. Being an artist, the expression is most important, whether it comes through writing or art. Saying things out is most important. I can feel the grief till my bones of how not tackling my ADHD head-on has led to so many problems, and I am still not done with it.

Last time I wrote about hyperfixation and what I am gonna do about it, and I think I am just beginning to deal with it. There is a deep desire to be so involved in my work that I forget everything, but at the same time it leads to disconnection, and a feeling of loneliness creeps in.

>> Being an artist, the expression is most important — whether it comes through writing or art.

It's a synchronicity: immediately after, in the book I read, that to become whole, to become truly individual, you don't become so by being alone. And that's so true. The metaphor of the woman drinking from a broken glass, which appeared as a dream, is so true for people with ADHD. You are getting nourished, but the glass is broken, and that's what the truth is. People around you should be aware that this is what happens to you. If you say that, it becomes a lot easier. Most of the grief that comes from being a person with ADHD requires a lot of communication.

>> You are getting nourished, but the glass is broken.

As I continue to speak about the grief of ADHD, I am realizing it has become so important to manage the hyperfixation, to be in control of everything. And there is the advice of getting out of hyperfixation, allowing imperfection to creep in, not being perfect all the time, and living with the insecurity. Those are the parts to be navigated well, because hyperfixation will lead you to insecurity, and it is needed also, because to produce good work, you need that attention.
