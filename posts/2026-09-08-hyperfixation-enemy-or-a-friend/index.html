Hyperfixation, Enemy or a Friend?

Sep 8, 2026 · 3 min read · Being Seen

Listen 0:00
a borrowed voice reads this (AI-generated narration)
ADHD hyperfixation is an intense, all-consuming focus on a specific hobby, topic, activity, or person that blocks out the outside world.

Today I'm going to talk about hyperfixation, an experience I went through last week while doing my typography experiment.

I was building a typography poster for one of the product ideas I'm working on, Stencil and Frame. I allowed myself to explore typography freely, starting with thumbnails and using AI to generate different combinations and compare them with my own ideas.

While doing this, I discovered that the sentence I was trying to compose had a lot of depth in itself. I realized that you can write a sentence and give it a visual meaning that someone has to decode.

You can write a sentence and give it a visual meaning that someone has to decode.

As I continued exploring, I somehow entered a zone that became more and more addictive. I wanted to make the layout increasingly difficult to read while experimenting with different visual styles. The depth of the work seemed to increase as I continued.

Eventually, I noticed that I kept attempting to make it better and better, and everything outside of it started feeling like a distraction. I didn't want to stop. For a long time, I didn't eat or drink water, and I kept working for hours until the end of the day.

Even after I went to sleep, ideas kept coming to me about how I could make it better. I felt like I shouldn't have stopped and should have continued working.

After coming out of this episode, I realized that hyperfixation helped me produce something I had never done before. At the same time, getting out of it was very difficult, and I needed a lot of rest afterwards.

While I was dysregulated, I also found it difficult to connect with people. I couldn't connect even with my own family members, and I started feeling increasingly guilty about it. I was irritated and disconnected, but at the same time, deep inside, my brain seemed to be trying to create patterns to get back to that state again.

More and more ideas kept spinning up about how I could make the work better. My mind kept creating new possibilities and connections, and I felt like I had very little control over it. Even though I knew I needed to step away, some part of my brain was still trying to return to the work and continue creating.

What confuses me about hyperfixation is that although I produced what I felt was my best outcome, I also felt that my nervous system had become dysregulated because I had given so much attention to the work without taking care of my body.

There was a strange contradiction between the joy I felt while creating the piece and the dysregulation I experienced afterwards.

I had to take a complete day to recover.

What I think about now is that I'm a little scared of getting caught in the same loop again. I think hyperfixation can be creatively powerful, but I need to make sure that I take rest, eat, drink water, and take care of my body so that the intensity doesn't turn into dysregulation.

Hyperfixation can be creatively powerful — as long as the intensity doesn't turn into dysregulation.
