A Breathing Widget That Doesn't Nag Me

Sep 23, 2026 · 2 min read · Being Seen

Listen 0:00
a borrowed voice reads this (AI-generated narration)

In my last post, I mentioned the app that I built for myself. I keep building it, mostly by coding in my free time, whenever I get a moment. It's very addictive to write code and build software for yourself.

While I do that, I developed this breathing functionality. I think one of the ways people with ADHD can regulate themselves with ease is by focusing on breathing. When I went to implement it, I wanted it to be very subtle, because I don't want something nudging me, poking me, "hey, breathe properly." That's too intrusive. So I worked with Claude and came up with this animation concept, which is always glowing. I tap on it and it changes the color. Something little and playful I built. It keeps changing the colors, and after 20 minutes it goes back to the neutral color.

I also built a guided meditation on the widget. I can press the play button and it guides me through box breathing. There's another kind of breathing that I do, where I exhale more, which helps in calming down. Something I learned from therapy, how to calm your mind. There are times when breathing really helps a lot. And there are times when you have deeper emotional issues, and then breathing doesn't help at all. I'm not sure how to design something for that. That's something I will probably develop some other day. Maybe a widget which asks you how you feel, which emotions are there, and lets you express them. That's a deeper problem to tackle later.

What I feel now is that software development has reached everyone. Now everyone can design an application for their own needs with ease, with a little knowledge of desktop-based development, like what GitHub is, what desktop applications and web applications are. Even people who have no coding background can code it, but definitely there is a learning curve involved. I think that's not too difficult, because right now Claude can drive all the things for you. Understanding pull requests and GitHub can help anyone go past that barrier. This is such a beautiful time to develop something hyper-specific for your hyper-specific need. That's one of the best things about AI: you can design something for exactly what you need.

Such a beautiful time to develop something hyper-specific for your hyper-specific need.

Another learning curve for people who are not familiar with coding would be to think in terms of user experience itself, what kind of user experience they want to build. That's a skill in itself: how much software you have used, how many patterns you remember. And you can always ask the AI what pattern will best fit here, and it can develop it for you, and then you have a choice whether you want to keep the feature or not.

I'm just loving this time of developing software for myself.
