Studio Zero. While building this field notes website, one idea keeps striking me: I have multiple interests apart from software development. Motion graphics, audiovisual, I can't stop myself from pursuing any of it. The more I try to stop, the more difficult it becomes.

Thinking through it this morning, doing a thinking exercise, I realized it would make far more sense to become a creative studio that does multiple things. Having a commercial aspect matters right now — if I can support myself commercially, that feeds back into my art, the cinema I want to create, the paintings I want to create. That feels like the right direction. Rather than investing everything into one area, betting that software development will succeed and this one product will become a success — a product may or may not succeed, and I need to be clear about that from day one. The moment you start projecting "this is THE project, the product I'm betting on," it becomes difficult, especially with ADHD.

Right now I've paused Stencil and Frame. I realized my relationship with that product had gotten so messed up that I was scared inside — whether it could even be built, what was at stake. To change that relationship and support my soul, I think the way forward is to think of myself as a creative agency — a one-person creative agency producing design and development, and showcasing case studies. Stencil and Frame becomes a case study where I did UX, where I did UI, and I showcase it as such. Similarly with motion design — if I build a typography piece for Stencil and Frame, I can highlight it as a case study for motion design, as part of the creative agency.

This is a big leap in thinking, so I want to take a break and start from ground zero — the studio name, not investing in fancy things like buying a domain, but producing work the world can actually buy. I think that shift is going to help me break away from the older mindset — the one that came from years spent developing software and assuming software is what pays the bills. But there are several opportunities I hadn't even considered. My mind opened up yesterday seeing videos that earn money — people making "study with me" videos, even those succeeding. There's a creative aspect to it too. I can score my own music and build videos like that. There's a lot out there I don't even know exists yet.

I think pursuing an independent studio will help my ADHD brain identify multiple opportunities rather than staying limited to one. So I'll keep pursuing this. And this website — speaking my thoughts out loud — has really helped me shape my thinking and give direction, supporting my ADHD brain.

As I applaud myself, the doubts always creep in. I want to give this a week's time and see where it drives me forward.
