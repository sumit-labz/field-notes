I wanted clarity on how to organize the way I work, so I can sense a direction. Right now, while I'm building skills in motion and typography, a feeling of emptiness keeps showing up — yeah, you're learning the skill, but in isolation, without a bigger picture. So I had an internal discussion with myself about what I'm actually doing.

I divided the work into two engines: build and distribute.

Build is where the software gets made — in this case, Studio Zero producing the Story Dojo software. Development needs domain knowledge — how stories work, John Truby's book on the anatomy of story. And there's a product side to it too: development can feel like just building an HTML app or a mobile app, but product thinking is about how you shape the UX and UI.

Distribute is the harder question. The whole idea is: what do we build, and how do we distribute it? The biggest challenge is creating distribution content that isn't only for distribution — that also becomes part of the product. Distribution has to produce artifacts, and those artifacts may or may not end up part of the build. If they do, that's great. If they don't, it's still a pure educational piece, and that's fine too. I think that's the distinction to hold onto.

Inside distribution, there are four pieces that have to work together: message, story, expression, and bridge.

Say the message is "how a premise works" — that could become a multi-part video. The story is how you explain the premise itself — examples from different movies, what their premise was, a script built around that. Expression is what you use to say it: typography, motion, diagrams, sound, voice — the visual aesthetic, which starts to shade into art direction. And the bridge is how it loops back to the product — as simple as showing, in five or ten seconds, how the software helps someone write a premise.

The question I still don't have an answer to: does the expression for distribution have to share the same art direction — the same visual DNA — as the product build? I don't know yet. But this gives me a structure to produce artifacts, and I think my job is to produce artifacts, pulling in whatever practice engine each one needs. Putting a diagram to this gave me a direction.

I spent more time on how build and distribute connect — so it feels like it's coming from one source, so the distribution content feels like it shares the software's DNA. One idea: decide the color palette and the typography once, and both sides will look like they come from the same family. Build and distribute can keep their own voices — distribution could be playful, or something else I decide later — the voices don't need to depend on each other. The only visual relationship they need is color and font, so they read as coming from one source.

So that's where I land for now: think from the product itself about how to decide the colors and the typography — more of an art direction question. I'll keep figuring that out.
