I want to take a note about the emotions I've been going through while I built this creative studio. Recently, I went through a hyper-fixation loop. It was for a long time, getting addicted to things I wanted to build. I should say it's more about discovering something so addictive that you have no control over it and you keep coming back. Sometimes it's building music, sometimes it's just creating a three-value scale thumbnail. You find something more interesting and then you get hyper-fixated on it.

Then you come out of it, and when your dopamine level falls off, you realize what you were doing. At the same time, you feel a grief for missing out on your connections, relationships. This year has been one of the most connected phases of my life, where I was feeling so connected with people, expressing myself. And I think I'm already missing that right now. Although morality doesn't play much role, deep inside I am always a people person. I want to feel excited about people, the way they are, howsoever imperfect they are. But the way life goes, how things change, sometimes you don't like behaviors of others, sometimes you like it. Relationship is so complex in itself. Generally I feel so good about people around, but when I get dysregulated, all the negative energies, negative thoughts come in about people and I have no control over it. And obviously this problem cannot be solved by morality.

On one side, there was a dysregulation due to hyper-fixation, and now I'm getting back. I have helped myself by giving myself a lot of rest and getting back in good energy, excited about music. Music is giving me such a huge excitement at this time, the sound of drums of the eighties. Somehow I feel I have a zeal to explore all this, how those sounds were created, the eighties synthesizer. This is what I'm excited about today. I'm already planning that this evening I will try to decode how great artists of the eighties used the synthesizer. And if I could produce any of those sounds in Ableton Live, that will be so amazing.

On the other hand, one of the problems that comes with ADHD is time blindness. So I thought, okay, let's look at the bigger picture today. On one side, there is distribution, which I'm developing my skills for, and on the other side there is development. Development is something I have a decent hold on, apart from putting up a process on UX and UI, which I have to figure out. But on the distribution side, I'm still building the skills, and I have chosen kinetic typography as the way of my expression. It's pretty exciting, how the camera moves, the text moves. It's a very exciting field. I really don't know where it will end, but I really want to go on this journey of kinetic typography and build something. I've chosen HTML as the path right now. I'm not sure about After Effects, but overall, I just want to take note of the journey and how I'm progressing, how things are going, without intentionally missing out on things.

I think I'm rambling now, but with an ADHD brain, reflection works so well. You need to reflect on your thoughts again and again. Only then will it make sense at some point. I recently started food logging, the food I'm eating these days. When I reflected on it, I realized I was consuming far more protein than I had been worrying about. So logging and reflecting will be the core part of my journey, and that will help me stay on the path.

>> With an ADHD brain, reflection works so well.

The self-doubt always creeps in because the interests are changing. You drop some journeys and you embark on new ones. Then you think, why did I drop that? Why did it become slow? Handling multiple things, learning multiple things, doing multiple things at the same time has a cost. And I think you've got to give yourself the benefit of the doubt also. I think that's what the message coming from inside is, as I speak. Don't be harsh on yourself when you're doing well. Probably I'm out of that hyper-fixation loop and I'm back at work with good energy.

>> Don't be harsh on yourself when you're doing well.
