Cognitive Surrender and Not Every Day Being a Sprint Day

Sep 16, 2026 · 2 min read · Being Seen

Listen 0:00
a borrowed voice reads this (AI-generated narration)

I'm reflecting on yesterday. I felt I went through a burnout, where I was trying really hard but nothing was happening. I was trying to get out of it, but my mind was locked onto whatever I was doing, and it was not moving anywhere. My logical brain was saying I should get out of it, but I wasn't able to. There was some realization of failure, that this was not going to work, and the brain kept trying hard, saying you can push through, you can try this, try that, and it would work. But it was not a very productive day.

Luckily, while I was browsing Instagram, somebody made a comment that in ADHD, not every day is a sprint day, and you should not come with the expectation that you will be able to sprint every day. That's similar to a hyperfixation day. Due to hyperfixation, you create something beyond your imagination, and you want that every day. Once you achieve the sprint or hyperfixation, you feel you can achieve higher every day. That expectation automatically creeps in. I have to shape up my mind differently, maybe by writing, speaking, what I'm doing right now, so that I don't expect a higher goal every day.

Not every day is a sprint day.

Another thing I observed yesterday, when I got dysregulated with the expectation of why today was not a high-performing day, is cognitive surrender while working with AI. With the help of AI, I was able to build a process to create the content. At some point I realized this process had become so awkward. Instead of AI asking me how to build this, it was suggesting everything. It kept suggesting one step after another, and I kept choosing. So I realized I had entered some kind of cognitive surrender mode, where I can't use my own brain at all. Whatever AI recommends, I choose it, because I think it's superior content, a superior choice.

When I woke up in the morning, my mind was gaining clarity. It was telling me that I need to own each and everything. AI cannot decide anything for me. I have to control the process. AI leads to cognitive surrender, where you think AI is much superior to you, and you have to build the skills in such a way that AI does not get you into the mode of making choices on your behalf. It's about developing skills in a way that AI doesn't control you. Those are some of the observations from yesterday.

AI cannot decide anything for you. You have to control the process.
